## Skills an entrepreneur needs

### 1. Networking

**Definition:** Networking is building relationships that bring you co-founders, investors, customers and advice.
**Class example:** No one builds a startup alone. *The Social Network* shows that the first team is not always the final one. Switch to people who can execute the vision.
**Business example:** Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin's stake was heavily diluted as the company grew and brought in experienced people.

### 2. Sales skills

**Class example:** You need sales skills for two reasons: to win customers and to pitch for funding.
**Business example:** Zomato's founders began by collecting restaurant menus in Delhi by hand and persuading restaurants to list. Selling to both restaurants and investors was part of the job from day one.

### 3. Self-belief

**Key idea:** Confidence keeps a founder going when results are slow.

### 4. Clear communication

**Class example:** A woman founder's case showed that communication was what got her company built.
**Business example:** Falguni Nayar left a senior banking job and started Nykaa at 50 in 2012. Her clear story of making beauty products accessible helped win investors and customers, and Nykaa listed in 2021.

### Disruptive versus innovative founders

**Class example:** Disruptive founders differ from merely innovative ones by knowing exactly what they want. Leaders should be flexible and admit vulnerability, and stakeholders need to be supportive.
**Business example:** Reliance Jio's 2016 launch with free voice calls and very cheap data changed how the whole Indian telecom market worked.
**Exam tip:** Disruption changes how a market works; innovation improves a product within it.

## GEM data

**Definition:** The Global Entrepreneurship Monitor is a yearly international study, started in 1999 by Babson College and London Business School, that surveys adults on starting and running businesses. The transcript heard it as "Global Answer Solution Monitor".
**Class example:** The data splits startups by sector (agriculture, services) and gender, and compares countries.

### Findings discussed

- India's business failure rate is well under half of some established markets. One African country (Angola was named) showed a surprisingly high figure.
- High-income countries and parts of the Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia) scored lower on how feasible people find starting a business. India scored positive.
- **Fear of failure is the main barrier.**
- About 30% would start again after a failure or exit. Exit rates were quoted at 4.5% and 2.5%, with team breakups a common cause.
- India, last 3 to 5 years: startup rates quoted as 12% for women and 11% for men (as heard; check against the report).
- On the 1 to 10 favourability scale, 5 and above is positive, and India scored 5+ on the listed factors.

**Remember:** These figures were heard in class through speech-to-text. Check exact numbers against the GEM report before quoting them in an exam.

### Why people start

**Definition:** Necessity entrepreneurship is starting because jobs are scarce. Opportunity entrepreneurship is starting to pursue a gap or make a difference.
**Class example:** Motives split between wanting to make a difference and scarce jobs. Family influence was quoted at "75% plus".
**Business example:** Many Indian family businesses, such as Haldiram's, show how strongly family shapes the decision to start and continue a business.

## Self-employment in India

**Definition:** Subsistence self-employment is working for yourself to earn a basic living, not to build a scalable company.
**Class example:** Gig workers such as plumbers call themselves self-employed because they are on no payroll.
**Business example:** Urban Company lists plumbers, electricians and beauticians as independent service partners. It gives them customers and training, but they remain self-employed.
**Exam tip:** Do not treat all self-employment as entrepreneurship. Separate subsistence work from growth-oriented ventures.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Name four skills an entrepreneur needs and give an example for one.
**A:** Networking, sales skills, self-belief and clear communication. Falguni Nayar's clear story about making beauty accessible helped her win investors and customers for Nykaa.

**Q2.** What is the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor, and what did it show about fear of failure?
**A:** GEM is a yearly international survey of entrepreneurship, started in 1999 by Babson College and London Business School. It found fear of failure to be the main barrier that stops people starting businesses.

**Q3.** Distinguish necessity from opportunity entrepreneurship.
**A:** Necessity entrepreneurship happens when jobs are scarce, so people start businesses to earn a living. Opportunity entrepreneurship happens when people see a gap or want to make a difference.

**Q4.** Why are sales skills important for a founder?
**A:** Founders must sell twice: to customers, who bring revenue, and to investors, who bring funding. Zomato's founders sold to restaurants and investors from the start.

**Q5.** Is a self-employed plumber an entrepreneur? Discuss.
**A:** In a broad sense yes, as they work for themselves. But most are subsistence self-employed, earning a living without building a scalable venture. Platforms like Urban Company give them customers, but they remain independent partners.

**Q6.** Differentiate a disruptive founder from an innovative one.
**A:** An innovative founder improves a product within a market; a disruptive founder changes how the market works. Reliance Jio's cheap data in 2016 reshaped Indian telecom as a whole.

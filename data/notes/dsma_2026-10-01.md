This class focused on building the logic for a website analytics project, specifically the conversion funnel, product detail page evaluation, and how to handle missing data when real company numbers are not available. The discussion moved from AI tool usage for the project to a detailed framework for mapping customer journey from awareness to post-purchase.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Class example |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Baseline level of whether a user knows an offering exists | Discussed as the starting point before engagement |
| Engagement | Active interaction and reach that follows awareness | Treated as a key KPI to show the user journey |
| Conversion | The point where a user's need is matched with the right product and a purchase happens | Earphones example, user must find the right product to convert |
| Funnel | A sequence of steps where the number of users reduces at each stage | 1,000 users at engagement, lower number at conversion |
| Product Detail Page (PDP) | The specific product page that drives the final purchase decision | Earbuds example, checked for size, warranty, reviews |
| Social proof | Ratings and reviews that build buyer trust | Volume and quality of reviews tracked on PDP |
| Conversion gap | The share of visitors who do not buy after viewing a product page | Stated as 70 percent non-conversion on PDP visits |
| VIP pattern | Behaviour of high value customers who add to cart repeatedly but do not buy | Used to identify priority customers for targeting |
| Information gap | Missing data that a manager must trace and fill from another source | Social media suggested as a source to fill internal data gaps |
| Ad Library | Facebook's public tool to view live and past ads of any page | Suggested by Santanu Paul to study competitor ads |

## Website Analytics Project Framework

### Website selection and documentation
**Definition:** The first task for the project is to choose one real website and prepare a document analysing it.
**Class example:** Students were told to research and finalise a website before building the funnel analysis.
**Exam tip:** If asked about project methodology, mention that the first step is always website selection followed by documentation of its structure and funnel.

### Awareness vs Engagement vs Conversion
**Definition:** Awareness is simply knowing an offering exists. Engagement is the active interaction that follows. Conversion is the actual completion of the desired action, usually a purchase.
**Class example:** The faculty used the earphones example, saying a web page exists to guide the user to the exact product that fits their need. If the user cannot find the right product, it is a conversion failure, not just a traffic problem.
**Business example:** Amazon India uses personalised recommendation and filter systems on its search and category pages so that a user searching for "wireless earbuds" is guided quickly to a matching product, reducing drop off between engagement and conversion.
**Remember:** Awareness feeds engagement, and engagement only becomes valuable if it ends in conversion.
**Exam tip:** Always present this as a sequence (Awareness → Engagement → Conversion) in diagram form when answering funnel questions.

### Conversion funnel logic
**Definition:** A funnel is a model showing how the number of users keeps reducing as they move through stages from initial reach to final purchase and beyond.
**Class example:** Faculty gave an illustrative number: 1,000 users at the engagement stage falling to a smaller number at conversion, to show the standard decay pattern expected in any funnel.
**Business example:** Flipkart's typical funnel moves from app visits to product views to cart additions to checkout, with each stage showing fewer users than the one before, a pattern e-commerce analysts use to find the weakest stage.
**Formula (textbook):** Conversion rate = (Number of users who convert / Number of users who entered that funnel stage) x 100
**Exam tip:** When asked to build a funnel for a project, always use assumed but logically decreasing numbers since real company data is proprietary and usually unavailable to students.

### Product Evaluation Framework
**Definition:** This framework looks at how a user moves from browsing a broad category to selecting one specific product, and what features of the site help or block this movement.
**Class example:** Three focus areas were named: inventory and discovery (number of products and information shown), interface flexibility (ease of filtering), and category navigation (how users move through categories to reach one product).
**Business example:** Myntra's filter system for size, colour, brand, and price lets a user narrow down thousands of listings to a short, relevant set, which is a direct application of interface flexibility.

### Product Detail Page (PDP) analysis
**Definition:** The PDP is the page for one specific product and is treated as the main driver of conversion once a user has narrowed down their choice.
**Class example:** Key performance areas (KPAs) listed were critical product data (size, warranty), social proof (ratings and reviews), and user engagement (time spent on page).
**Business example:** Boat (the Indian earphones and wearables brand) displays size charts, warranty terms, and thousands of reviews directly on its PDP to reduce the hesitation that causes drop off.
**Remember:** 70 percent of users who visit a PDP do not convert, according to the number given in class. Time spent on the PDP is read as a positive signal of purchase intent, not a negative one.
**Exam tip:** If a question asks why conversion rate is low despite high traffic, use this 70 percent figure and explain it as an evaluation stage drop off, not a traffic problem.

### Information gap analysis
**Definition:** A manager's core job in analytics is to spot where data is missing, understand why it is missing, and identify an alternate source to fill that gap.
**Class example:** Social media was named as a primary alternate source when internal company data is not available to students for the project.
**Business example:** When companies do not share their internal cart or payment data publicly, analysts often use social media engagement data and public ad libraries instead, as was suggested for the Facebook Ad Library.

### Customer profiling by function
**Definition:** The same website data can be read differently depending on which department is using it.
**Class example:** Faculty mentioned Finance and Marketing as two different functional profiles that would look at the same customer and fund investment behaviour differently, along with the idea of segmenting website views by group.
**Business example:** In a company like Zerodha, the Marketing team may look at website visits as leads to convert, while the Finance team looks at the same visits in terms of which investment products or funds are attracting money.

### Data sourcing for the project
**Definition:** Since real company numbers are usually confidential, the project must rely on a mix of assumed numbers and publicly available data types.
**Class example:**
1. Transaction data and preliminary databases were named as sources for metrics.
2. Companies typically do share data on promotions and how they are run.
3. Companies rarely share granular data, such as exact cart contents.
4. VIP or high priority customers can be identified by a behaviour pattern: users who frequently add items to cart but never complete the purchase.
**Business example:** Many D2C brands in India run cart abandonment email or WhatsApp campaigns specifically targeting users who show this add-to-cart-but-no-purchase pattern, treating them as high intent VIP leads.

### Post-purchase experience
**Definition:** The customer journey does not end at the transaction. The post-purchase phase (delivery, support, returns) is part of the overall customer experience that analytics should also track.
**Class example:** Faculty stated this directly while discussing UX strategy for the project, saying the journey continues beyond the sale.
**Business example:** Myntra and Flipkart both track post-purchase metrics like return rate and delivery satisfaction as part of their overall customer experience score, not just the checkout conversion number.

### Ad Library for competitor analysis
**Definition:** The Facebook Ad Library is a free public tool that shows all currently running and past ads of any Facebook or Instagram page, useful for analysing competitor advertising strategy.
**Class example:** Santanu Paul suggested using it to extract data on current ads running for the chosen website or brand. Faculty noted that payment gateway details and similar information cannot be captured through the ad library and need a manual visit to the website itself.
**Business example:** Digital marketers routinely use the Facebook Ad Library to study how competitors like Nykaa or Lenskart position their ad creatives and offers before planning their own campaign.

## What the faculty stressed

- Define every identified "gap" clearly: explain exactly why a piece of data or placement is not matching, rather than noting it vaguely.
- The web page's job is to match the user to the exact product they need. Conversion fails when that match fails, not just when traffic is low.
- Since real company data will not be available for the student project, use logical assumed numbers that still follow the correct funnel decay pattern.
- Time spent on a product detail page should be read as a positive signal of buying intent, an opportunity, not a warning sign.
- Always approach the website analysis from the customer's own journey perspective, including what happens after the purchase is made.
- A manual visit to the actual website is necessary for details like payment gateways, since these cannot be captured by the Ad Library or automated tools.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Differentiate between awareness, engagement, and conversion as discussed in class, using the earphones example.
**A:** Awareness is simply the user knowing the product or website exists. Engagement is the active browsing and interaction that follows, such as searching or filtering. Conversion happens only when the user finds the exact product matching their need and completes the purchase, as shown in the earphones example where failure to find the right product means conversion has failed even if engagement was high.

**Q2.** Explain the Product Evaluation Framework and its three focus areas.
**A:** This framework tracks how a user narrows down from a broad category to one specific product. The three areas are inventory and discovery (range and information about products available), interface flexibility (how easily a user can filter choices), and category navigation (how users move through categories to reach their target product). Myntra's filter system for size and price is a good real world example of interface flexibility.

**Q3.** What is the significance of the 70 percent figure mentioned for Product Detail Pages?
**A:** It refers to the conversion gap, meaning 70 percent of users who visit a product detail page do not end up buying. Faculty framed this not as a loss but as an opportunity, since the users visiting are already showing intent. Increased time on the page is seen as a positive signal that the user is actively evaluating the product, which analysts can use to design better triggers for conversion.

**Q4.** How should a student handle the lack of real company data while building the funnel for the class project?
**A:** Since actual company numbers are usually proprietary, the project should use assumed numbers that still follow the logical funnel decay pattern, for example 1,000 users at engagement reducing to a smaller number at conversion. Available public data types like promotion details can be used, but granular data like exact cart contents should be estimated rather than expected from the company.

**Q5.** What is a VIP pattern in customer behaviour and how can it be identified?
**A:** A VIP pattern refers to high priority customers identified through a specific behaviour, typically users who repeatedly add items to their cart but never complete the purchase. This pattern signals high interest without conversion, making these users good targets for retargeting campaigns such as cart abandonment offers.

**Q6.** Why is the Facebook Ad Library useful for this project, and what are its limitations?
**A:** The Ad Library lets you see all current and past ads run by a brand's Facebook or Instagram page, which is useful for analysing competitor advertising strategy, as suggested by Santanu Paul. Its limitation is that it cannot show details like payment gateway setup or other on-site operational details, which require a manual visit to the actual website.

**Q7.** Explain why post-purchase experience matters in website analytics, even though the transaction is already complete.
**A:** The customer journey does not stop once the payment is made. Faculty stressed that post-purchase elements like delivery and support are still part of the overall customer experience and should be tracked. Companies that ignore this phase risk losing repeat customers even if their initial conversion numbers look strong.

**Q8.** How do Finance and Marketing teams typically use the same customer data differently, based on the class discussion?
**A:** The same website or transaction data can be read through different functional profiles. A Marketing profile would look at the data to judge lead quality and campaign performance, while a Finance profile would look at the same customer behaviour in terms of fund investment and revenue impact. This shows why segmenting data by department or use case matters in analytics.

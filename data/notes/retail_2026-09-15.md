## Retail as part of marketing

**Key idea:** Opening outlets is a growth decision. Treat retail development as an extension of marketing strategy.
**Class example:** Assessments will include case studies where you apply retail concepts.

## Entering a market without your own brand

**Class example:** A new entrant cannot offer every service on day one. Without its own brand, it should negotiate with established brands for a wider assortment (Apollo water bottles were the example). If prices are too high, locals skip it and buy from the brand directly.
**Business example:** When DMart started in 2002 it had no brand pull of its own. It won shoppers by stocking trusted national brands at consistently low prices, then added private labels later.

## Classifying retail formats

1. **By ownership:** independent, chain, franchise.
2. **By location:** high street, mall, neighbourhood.
3. **By pricing:** premium, value, discount.
4. **By channel:** traditional versus modern.

| Format | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kirana | Neighbourhood shop, small space, monthly credit, home delivery | Local stores |
| EBO | Sells one brand only | Zara, Nike, Levi's, Puma |
| MBO | Many brands under one roof | Lifestyle, Shoppers Stop, Trends |
| Department store | Split into departments | Shoppers Stop |
| Supermarket | Self-service groceries | DMart |
| Hypermarket | Very large, groceries plus general goods | Reliance Smart, earlier Big Bazaar |
| Convenience store | Within 2 to 3 km of homes | 24Seven |
| Specialty store | One category | Nykaa, Sephora (beauty); Croma, Reliance Digital (electronics) |
| Cash and carry | Bulk sales to businesses | Metro Cash and Carry |
| Factory outlet | Discounted stock | Brand outlets |
| Pop-up | Temporary store | Event stalls |

**Remember:** Variety sells more. A shopper who came for one shirt leaves with two when colours and styles are wide. Customer segmentation decides how the store is laid out.
**Business example:** Big Bazaar, run by Future Group, was India's best-known hypermarket. After Future Group's debt crisis in 2020 to 2022, Reliance Retail took over many of its store locations.
**Business example:** Trent runs Zara in India through a joint venture with Inditex, while its own Westside and Zudio stores sell only Trent's own labels.
**Exam tip:** Classify formats on at least two bases (ownership and pricing) with one example each. It shows structured thinking.

## Consumer shifts: omnichannel and Marketing 5.0

**Definition:** Omnichannel means a customer can move smoothly between online and offline channels with one brand.
**Definition:** Webrooming is researching online, then buying in a store. Showrooming is the reverse: seeing a product in a store, then buying online.
**Class example:** The class described browsing in store and buying online as "webrooming". That is actually showrooming, so check your notes.
**Business example:** Lenskart lets you choose frames online, try them at home or in a store, and get an eye test in store. That is omnichannel design.

**Definition:** Marketing 5.0, from Philip Kotler's 2021 book, is using technology such as AI to serve people better.
**Class example:** The same brand varies in quality and price across China, Germany, the US and India.

## AI adoption in retail

**Class example:** AI works as a chain, where one AI system calls another. It differs from simple automation or robotics. Two adoption models were discussed: fully trusting AI output, and AI with human guidance.
**Business example:** Amazon's product recommendations are a well-known retail use of AI. McKinsey has estimated that about 35% of what customers buy on Amazon comes from recommendations.
**Business example:** Sephora's Virtual Artist lets shoppers try make-up shades on their own face through augmented reality before buying.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Classify retail formats with one Indian example each.
**A:** Kirana (neighbourhood shop), EBO (Zara), MBO (Trends), department store (Shoppers Stop), supermarket (DMart), hypermarket (Reliance Smart), convenience store (24Seven), specialty store (Nykaa), cash and carry (Metro), factory outlet and pop-up store.

**Q2.** Distinguish webrooming from showrooming.
**A:** Webrooming is researching online and buying in a store. Showrooming is seeing a product in a store and buying it online, often for a lower price.

**Q3.** How can a new retailer compete without its own brand?
**A:** It can stock trusted national brands, negotiate for a wide assortment and price competitively so locals do not go to the brand directly. DMart started this way before adding private labels.

**Q4.** Explain omnichannel retail with an example.
**A:** Omnichannel lets customers move between online and offline channels with one brand. Lenskart lets you choose frames online, try them at home or in store, and take an eye test in the store.

**Q5.** Give two uses of AI in retail.
**A:** Product recommendations, as on Amazon, where McKinsey estimated about 35% of purchases come from recommendations; and virtual try-on, as with Sephora's AR make-up tool.

**Q6.** What happened to Big Bazaar, and what does it teach about retail?
**A:** Future Group's debt crisis from 2020 to 2022 left it unable to run Big Bazaar, and Reliance Retail took over many store locations. Footfall alone cannot save a format with weak finances.

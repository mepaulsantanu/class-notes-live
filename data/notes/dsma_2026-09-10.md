This first D&SMA class, with Prof Bindhia Joji, taught you to read data visuals, trace why traffic does not turn into sales, tell KPIs from metrics and analysis from analytics, and build dashboards that tell a story. It ended with a delivery-delay dataset exercise.

| --- | --- | --- |

## Reading any data visual

1. Understand what the chart shows.
2. Interpret what it means.
3. Decide the action.

**Class example:** Use a line chart for a trend over months or quarters, such as low in Q1 rising to Q4. Use a bar chart when data is uneven across categories.
**Class example:** If page views rise but conversions stay low, open a different visual to find where people drop off.
**Exam tip:** Always end a chart interpretation with an action. "Views rose 40%" is analysis; "move the offer higher on the page" is what earns marks.

## Conversion on Instagram and e-commerce

**Definition:** Conversion is when a visitor takes the action you want, such as a purchase. Conversion rate = conversions ÷ visitors.
**Class example:** An organic Instagram post cannot link to a landing page, so you need a paid ad for that. One click to the landing page is one visitor, and conversion is the move from that click to checkout.
**Business example:** Brands put a single link in their Instagram bio, or use link stickers in Stories, because feed posts do not carry clickable links. Paid ads are the main way to send feed viewers straight to a product page.
**Remember:** Traffic, time spent and engagement mean nothing if no money comes in.

### Why traffic does not convert

1. High cart abandonment.
2. No cash-on-delivery option.
3. A checkout page that is not simple.

**Business example:** The Baymard Institute puts average online cart abandonment at about 70%. Amazon's 1-Click ordering, patented in 1999, was built to cut checkout steps.
**Business example:** Flipkart, Myntra and Meesho keep cash on delivery because many Indian shoppers, especially first-time buyers, do not trust paying online upfront.
**Key idea:** The simpler the checkout, the higher the conversion.

### What to track in GA4

**Definition:** GA4 (Google Analytics 4) is Google's current analytics tool. It replaced Universal Analytics in July 2023.
**Class example:** Track cost per order, customer lifetime value (CLV), e-commerce events, and financial metrics from the point of sale onward.
**Formula:** CLV = average order value × purchases per year × years the customer stays (textbook).

## KPIs versus metrics

**Definition:** A KPI is a performance measure linked to a goal. A metric is any number that quantifies something.
**Class example:** Offline conversion rate = buyers ÷ walk-ins. Pull it from the CRM or count manually each week.
**Business example:** A store with 500 walk-ins and 50 buyers has a 10% conversion rate. Retail chains such as Lenskart track store conversion to judge staff and layout.
**Exam tip:** Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI. Page views are a metric; conversion rate against a target is a KPI.

## Analysis versus analytics

**Definition:** Analysis turns past and present data into visuals to understand what happened. Analytics uses data to predict what will happen.
**Class example:** Power BI is an analysis tool, not an analytics tool. Analytics 1.0 has moved on; GA4 is the current standard to explore.
**Class example:** In the class case, version 1.0 tracked only logins. Version 2.0 tracked time spent and how users moved from stage to stage.
**Business example:** Netflix uses analytics to predict what you will watch next and to decide which shows to commission.

## Building a dashboard

1. Start with the report.
2. Do the analysis (pivot tables work well).
3. Build the dashboard last.

**Definition:** A dashboard is a visual summary of KPIs that tells a story and points to action.
**Class example:** Use visuals, not tables. Aim for about two KPIs per page. Show where the process falls short and recommend a fix. Compare against a threshold: if conversion was 50% last year, explain why this year differs.
**Exam tip:** A good dashboard answers three questions: what happened, why, and what we do next.

## Dataset exercise: delivery delays

**Class example:** Amazon uploads transactions daily or next day depending on volume. You tested whether delays came from particular delivery staff, warehouses or product types. The dataset was inconsistent, which made cleaning part of the task.
**Business example:** Delhivery and Amazon track delivery performance by hub and courier so they can fix the weakest link rather than guess.
**Remember:** When you prompt AI to analyse data, name the framework you want it to use.

## What the faculty stressed

- Read, interpret, act.
- Engagement without conversion is not success.
- Simplify checkout to lift conversion.
- Dashboards must tell a story and recommend action.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Differentiate a KPI from a metric with an example.
**A:** A metric is any number, such as page views. A KPI is a metric tied to a goal, such as conversion rate against a 3% target. Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI.

**Q2.** Give three reasons why website traffic may not turn into sales.
**A:** High cart abandonment, no cash-on-delivery option, and a complicated checkout page. Average online cart abandonment is about 70%, so simplifying checkout often gives the biggest gain.

**Q3.** Distinguish analysis from analytics.
**A:** Analysis studies past and present data to explain what happened, for example charts in Power BI. Analytics uses data to predict what will happen, such as Netflix predicting what a user will watch next.

**Q4.** A store gets 800 walk-ins and 64 buyers in a week. Calculate and interpret the conversion rate.
**A:** 64 ÷ 800 = 8%. The manager should compare it with past weeks or a target and look at staff, layout or stock if it is falling.

**Q5.** What makes a good dashboard?
**A:** It uses visuals, shows about two KPIs per page, compares results against a threshold, and tells a story that ends in a recommendation. It comes after the report and analysis stages.

**Q6.** Why do Indian e-commerce firms keep cash on delivery?
**A:** Many Indian buyers, especially first-time online shoppers, do not trust paying upfront. Removing COD would raise abandonment, so Flipkart and Myntra keep it to protect conversion.

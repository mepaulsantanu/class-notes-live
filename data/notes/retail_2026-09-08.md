## Pricing psychology

**Definition:** Psychological pricing sets a price just under a round figure, because buyers read the left-most digit first and feel the product is cheaper.
**Class example:** ₹1,999 feels much cheaper than ₹2,000, though the gap is ₹1.
**Business example:** Bata has priced shoes at figures like ₹499 and ₹999 for decades. The ".99" ending became part of how Indian shoppers read Bata prices as good value.
**Remember:** Marketing works on human psychology to drive spending. Pricing is often the first place that psychology shows up.

## Case: the Indian mobile phone market

### Liberalisation opened the door

**Definition:** Liberalisation means removing controls so private and foreign firms can enter a market.
**Class example:** After the 1991 reforms under Dr Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister, global brands such as Nokia entered India. The class placed Nokia's rise around 1997 to 1998.
**Business example:** India's first mobile phone call was made on 31 July 1995, on a Nokia handset, between West Bengal Chief Minister Jyoti Basu and Telecom Minister Sukh Ram. Nokia went on to lead the Indian handset market for over a decade.

### Crowded wholesale and the value segment

**Class example:** Delhi's Sadar Bazar shows how crowded and price-driven wholesale markets are.
**Class example:** Micromax entered with cheap phones and grew into rural India. The phone repair market alone is worth crores.
**Business example:** Micromax briefly became one of India's top smartphone sellers around 2014, then lost share quickly once Chinese brands such as Xiaomi offered better specifications at similar prices. Value alone did not protect it.
**Exam tip:** Use Nokia and Micromax together to show two retail lessons: entry after liberalisation, and why a pure price position is fragile.

## Why Indian consumption is growing: four drivers

1. **Innovation** in products and services.
2. **Social media marketing** that reaches wider audiences.
3. **Rising investment** in the sector.
4. **Easy consumer finance**, through EMIs, UPI and Google Pay.

**Definition:** Consumer finance is credit that lets buyers spread payment over time.
**Class example:** EMIs make a scooter affordable. A hospital lets a patient finance the unpaid ₹5,000 of a ₹10,000 bill so they can leave hours earlier.
**Business example:** UPI, launched by NPCI in 2016, made instant payments normal even at small kiranas. Bajaj Finance built a large business on EMI cards used at electronics and appliance stores, turning big purchases into small monthly payments.
**Remember:** Payment ease is a retail growth driver in its own right. Fewer steps to pay means more completed sales.

## Store types introduced

**Definition:** A mono-brand store sells only one brand. Full coverage came on 15 Sep.
**Business example:** India has allowed up to 100% foreign investment in single-brand retail through the automatic route since 2018. IKEA opened its first Indian store in Hyderabad the same year.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** What is psychological pricing? Give a class example and a real brand example.
**A:** It is pricing just below a round number so the product feels cheaper, because buyers read the first digit. In class, ₹1,999 felt far cheaper than ₹2,000. Bata's long use of prices like ₹499 and ₹999 is a real example.

**Q2.** Explain how the 1991 reforms changed Indian retail, using the mobile phone market.
**A:** Liberalisation allowed global brands to enter India. Nokia came in and dominated handsets for over a decade. The open market also let low-cost players like Micromax grow into rural India, which widened retail reach.

**Q3.** List the four drivers of Indian consumption growth discussed in class.
**A:** Innovation in products and services, social media marketing, rising investment, and easy consumer finance. The last includes EMIs and instant payments such as UPI and Google Pay.

**Q4.** Why is a pure low-price position risky for a retailer or brand? Use Micromax.
**A:** A low price is easy to copy. Micromax grew on cheap phones but lost share quickly when Xiaomi and others offered better specifications at similar prices. Without another advantage, price alone could not hold customers.

**Q5.** How does consumer finance increase retail sales? Give two examples.
**A:** It lets buyers spread cost over time, so bigger purchases become affordable now. Bajaj Finance EMI cards turn appliances into monthly payments, and a hospital financing part of a bill lets a patient pay later.

## Self-discovery and the Johari Window

### The P&C sheet

The sheet (P&C means Private and Confidential) asked for your name story, a 2030 vision (where you will live, what work you will do, your personal life), a mania and a phobia, a role model, the person you value most, your greatest expectation from life and your biggest regret.

**Remember:** A mania is a harmless, irrational action you repeat often and others notice. A phobia is an irrational fear. The faculty said everyone has both.

### Johari Window

**Definition:** A model by Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham (1955) that splits self-knowledge into four panes: Open (known to you and others), Blind (known to others, not you), Hidden (known to you only) and Unknown (known to no one until something triggers it).
**Class example:** You wrote Part C, the hidden self, including one negative trait. The film *Secret Superstar* was used to show a hidden talent that surfaces only when triggered.
**Business example:** Brands use the same idea in research. Unilever's Dove "Real Beauty Sketches" (2013) showed that women described themselves far more harshly than strangers did, a "blind" gap the campaign then spoke to.
**Exam tip:** Link the Johari Window to consumer research: focus groups surface the Open pane, while projective techniques and observation reach the Blind and Hidden panes.

## Lateral thinking in advertising

**Definition:** Lateral thinking, a term coined by Edward de Bono, means solving a problem by changing the angle of approach instead of following the obvious step-by-step logic. Linear thinking fails in imaginative tests such as JEE Advanced or CAT.
**Class example:** Lateral inversion (a mirror image) shows how perspective changes what you see. "A hen is just an egg's way of making another egg."

### Ads discussed in class

1. **Sleepwell:** "Did you sleep well?" ties the brand name to the benefit without listing features or price.
2. **Cadbury 5 Star, "Do Nothing":** celebrates doing nothing, the opposite of what chocolate ads usually promise.
3. **Avis, "We're No. 2, so we try harder":** turns second place into a promise of better service.
4. **"Smoking reduces weight":** dark humour, because the weight you lose is your lungs.

**Business example:** Avis ran "We try harder" from 1962 with agency DDB. The company had been losing money for years and turned profitable within about a year of the campaign, and the line stayed in use for decades.
**Business example:** Amul's topical ads with the Amul girl, running since 1966, use lateral wordplay on current news every week. The brand gets huge recall on a small budget because each ad is a fresh angle on something people already talk about.

## Income versus standard of living

**Definition:** Income is personal, cognitive and invisible. Standard of living is social, behavioural and visible. The split maps to the ABC model of attitude: Affective (feel), Behavioural (do), Cognitive (know).
**Class example:** The Nawab of Lucknow who, though poor, still dressed in full regalia, carrying groundnuts in one pocket for himself and cashews in the other for guests.
**Key idea:** Marketing delivers a standard of living to society. Advertising shows that standard and makes people aspire to it.
**Business example:** Apple sells iPhones in India with no-cost EMI offers through bank partners. The monthly instalment lets a buyer display a premium standard of living that their income alone could not pay for upfront.
**Exam tip:** When a question asks why aspirational ads work, use the income versus SOL split: ads sell the visible SOL, and finance options close the gap with invisible income.

## Consumer behaviour concepts (Lecture 3 quiz)

### Brain and involvement

**Definition:** The right brain was described as the stronger but passive side, which takes in ads without effort.
**Business example:** Herbert Krugman's research on television (1965) found that people learn from TV ads with very low involvement. This is why brands like Fevicol rely on memorable, humorous visuals rather than arguments.

### Needs and motives

**Definition:** Homeostasis is the drive to balance your current state with your desired state. Sociogenic needs come from society or peers; psychogenic needs are the other, inner ones.
**Class example:** Hunger creates a gap that pushes you to eat.
**Business example:** Snickers' "You're not you when you're hungry" campaign (from 2010) sells a fix for exactly that gap between hungry and normal.

### Conspicuous consumption

**Definition:** Buying luxury to show others you can afford it. Economist Thorstein Veblen coined the term in 1899.
**Business example:** Large visible logos on brands such as Louis Vuitton and Gucci exist partly for this reason. The logo is the product's social signal.

### Personality determinants

**Definition:** Pre-natal determinants of personality form before birth. An ectomorph has a lean, thin build.

## The persona task

**Definition:** A persona (from the Latin for "mask") is a semi-fictional profile of one type of consumer: likes, dislikes, habits and how simple or complex their life is.
**Class example:** You made an AI or real image of yourself as a consumer, titled it with an adjective starting with your first letter, and posted it by 3:50 pm. Feedback on the drafts said to make the tone quirky and authentic, add shades of grey rather than a perfect picture, and use genuine adjectives.
**Remember:** Do not write "Gen Z consumer". Refine your AI prompt several times instead of accepting the first result.
**Business example:** Marketing teams build personas before planning campaigns. Software company HubSpot offers a free "Make My Persona" tool because a campaign aimed at one clear person is easier to write than one aimed at everyone.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Explain the four panes of the Johari Window and link each to a consumer research method.
**A:** The Open pane is what you and others know, reached through surveys and focus groups. The Blind pane is what others see but you do not, reached through observation. The Hidden pane is what you know but keep private, reached through anonymous or projective techniques. The Unknown pane surfaces only through triggers, such as in-depth interviews or experiments.

**Q2.** Using the Avis campaign, explain lateral thinking in advertising.
**A:** Linear logic says a brand should hide that it is second. Avis flipped the weakness into a promise: being No. 2 means it must try harder. The new angle made a negative fact the reason to choose the brand, which is lateral thinking.

**Q3.** Distinguish income from standard of living and explain why advertising focuses on the latter.
**A:** Income is personal, invisible and cognitive, such as salary. Standard of living is social, visible and behavioural, such as the car you drive. Advertising sells the visible standard because that is what people aspire to and display, and credit options like EMI close the gap with income.

**Q4.** What is conspicuous consumption? Give one brand example.
**A:** It is buying luxury goods mainly to show others you can afford them, a term from Thorstein Veblen. Louis Vuitton's visible monogram works as a social signal, so part of what the buyer pays for is being seen.

**Q5.** Define homeostasis and show how a brand uses it.
**A:** Homeostasis is the drive to close the gap between your current state and your desired state. Snickers frames hunger as that gap in "You're not you when you're hungry" and offers the bar as the way back to normal.

**Q6.** Why should a marketer build a consumer persona, and what mistakes should be avoided?
**A:** A persona gives the team one clear person to write for, which sharpens message and channel choices. Avoid vague labels like "Gen Z consumer", include real contradictions and dislikes, and refine the profile several times rather than accepting a first draft.

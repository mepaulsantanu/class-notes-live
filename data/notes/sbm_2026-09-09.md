This SBM class studied why brands succeed, fail and recover. It covered CCD's turnaround, the drivers of brand success, first-mover advantage and its limits, product-level versus strategic-level success, and famous mistakes such as New Coke and a breakfast brand's entry into South India.

| Term | Meaning | Class example |

| Brand turnaround | Recovering a damaged or failing brand | CCD after 2019 |

## Branding recap

**Key idea:** Branding is about credibility and trust, not packaging. People, places, ideas, memories and NGOs can all be branded.
**Class example:** Tourism places such as Dandeli and the Sundarbans can be branded as destinations.

## Brand turnaround: CCD

**Definition:** A brand turnaround is restoring a damaged brand's finances and reputation.
**Class example:** The speaker was transcribed as "Aishwarya", but the facts match Malavika Hegde at Coffee Day. Debt was cut from about ₹7,000 crore to about ₹465 crore, the vending-machine business was scaled, and costs were cut without hurting staff.
**Business example:** After founder V G Siddhartha's death in 2019, Malavika Hegde became CEO of Coffee Day Enterprises. She sold assets such as the tech park stake, paid down lenders and kept the cafe chain running.
**Exam tip:** Structure a turnaround answer in three steps: stop the bleeding (debt and costs), protect the core (stores and staff), then grow a strength (vending machines).

## Drivers of brand success

1. **A clear USP** comes first.
2. **Quality** that customers can rely on.
3. **Alignment with market trends**, as Tata does.
4. **A vision** for where the brand is going.

**Remember:** Trends alone do not guarantee success. A brand still needs a clear reason to exist.
**Class example:** Nike stands out through clear differentiation, which is strategic-level success.
**Business example:** Nike built its identity around the athlete in everyone. Its ads rarely talk about shoe features; they talk about effort.

## First-mover advantage and its limits

**Definition:** First-mover advantage is the benefit of entering a market first: brand recall, early customers and time to build distribution.
**Class example:** It helps you hold a market longer but does not guarantee success. The transcript cites a pioneer ("Golden Disc", likely misheard) losing to Bajaj on price.
**Business example:** Orkut was India's most-used social network until about 2010, then Facebook overtook it and Google shut Orkut in 2014. Being first did not protect it once it stopped improving.
**Business example:** Hindustan Motors' Ambassador led Indian cars for decades, then lost almost all share after Maruti launched the 800 in 1983.
**Exam tip:** Always pair first-mover advantage with the pioneer's trap. Examiners want both sides.

## Product-level versus strategic-level success

**Definition:** Product-level success comes from features or emotional appeal. Strategic-level success comes from a clear, lasting position in the customer's mind.
**Class example:** Clinic Plus built emotion through the mother-daughter bond in its ads.
**Business example:** Clinic Plus has used mother-daughter stories for years and became one of India's largest shampoo brands by volume, partly through low-priced sachets.

## Regional differences and market research

**Class example:** North and South India behave as different markets. A breakfast brand entered the South without research. It ignored joint families, local language and mothers' focus on nutrition, then had to reposition itself as a healthy breakfast.
**Business example:** This matches Kellogg's entry into India in 1994. Indians poured hot milk on cornflakes, which made them soggy, and traditional breakfasts were hot, cooked meals. Kellogg's later pushed health and nutrition messages and launched local variants.
**Exam tip:** Use Kellogg's as the classic example of entering a market without understanding local habits.

## New Coke

**Class example:** Coca-Cola changed its formula and later brought back "Coke Classic". The transcript says "Norway", but this is the 1985 New Coke case.
**Business example:** In April 1985 Coca-Cola replaced its formula with a sweeter "New Coke" after blind taste tests favoured it. Customer anger was so strong that the original returned as "Coca-Cola Classic" after 79 days. People were attached to the brand as much as to the taste.
**Remember:** Taste tests measure the product; they do not measure the emotional bond with the brand.

## What the faculty stressed

- Study why brands fail as closely as why they succeed.
- A clear USP is the first driver of success.
- First-mover advantage fades without innovation.
- Research local habits before entering a new region.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** What are the drivers of brand success discussed in class?
**A:** A clear USP, consistent quality, alignment with market trends, and a vision. Trends alone are not enough without a clear reason for the brand to exist.

**Q2.** Explain first-mover advantage and its limits with an Indian example.
**A:** Being first gives recall, early customers and time to build distribution. But leaders that stop innovating lose. Orkut led Indian social networking until about 2010 and then lost to Facebook, closing in 2014.

**Q3.** What went wrong with New Coke, and what does it teach brand managers?
**A:** Coca-Cola replaced its formula in 1985 based on taste tests, but loyal customers were attached to the original brand. After strong backlash, Coca-Cola Classic returned in 79 days. Emotional attachment can matter more than measured taste.

**Q4.** Why did Kellogg's struggle when it entered India?
**A:** It ignored local habits. Indians poured hot milk on cornflakes, which turned them soggy, and preferred hot cooked breakfasts. The brand had to reposition around health and nutrition and add local variants.

**Q5.** Distinguish product-level success from strategic-level success.
**A:** Product-level success comes from features or emotional appeal, as with Clinic Plus's mother-daughter ads. Strategic-level success comes from a clear, lasting position, as with Nike's focus on effort and the athlete in everyone.

**Q6.** Outline the steps of CCD's turnaround.
**A:** Debt was cut from about ₹7,000 crore to about ₹465 crore through asset sales and payments to lenders. The core cafe business and staff were protected, and the vending-machine business was scaled as a growth engine.

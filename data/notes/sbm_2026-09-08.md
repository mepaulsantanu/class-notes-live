This opening SBM class, taught by Prof Barkathunissa, laid out the five course units and the evaluation plan, then built a clear idea of what a brand is. It used founder stories from Apple, Starbucks and Nike, and ended with activities showing that anything, from people to countries, can be branded.

## Course plan and evaluation

### Five units

1. **Basics of branding:** product versus brand, success (Apple, Samsung, Coca-Cola, Dabur, Cadbury, Amrutanjan), failure and myopia (BlackBerry, Kodak), recovery (Maggi), pioneer risk and redefining markets (Nirma versus Ariel and Surf Excel, Bajaj Chetak).
2. **Brand equity:** quantitative (money value) and qualitative (psychological value).
3. **Brand identity:** naming types (acronyms like KFC and HUL, abstract names, nature names like Apple), taglines, and David and Jennifer Aaker's models.
4. **Brand extension and portfolio:** Kingfisher stretching into airlines, parent and sub-brands (Dove), brand hierarchy.
5. **Brand personality and rebranding:** mascots, founders as faces, symbols, celebrity risk, repositioning (UTI Bank to Axis Bank).

### Evaluation

- **CIA 1:** conceptual mapping plus customer research, possibly with sentiment analysis or decision trees.
- **CIA 3:** field visit. Pick a brand (Crocs was the example), find gaps in its portfolio and propose a redesign.
- Individual class participation. Long cases planned on Disney and Park Hotels.
- Main text: Kevin Lane Keller, *Strategic Brand Management*. Kotler is secondary.

## What a brand is

**Definition:** A brand is intangible: the image and emotion in a customer's mind that sets one producer's product apart from competitors. The American Marketing Association defines it as a name, term, sign, symbol or design that identifies a seller's goods and separates them from rivals (textbook).
**Class example:** Herders burned marks on cattle or coloured horns so they could tell their animals from a neighbour's. That is where "branding" comes from.
**Business example:** Tata has built trust over more than 150 years. When Tata Motors launched the Nexon EV, buyers trusted the car partly because of the parent name.
**Exam tip:** Always separate product (what the company makes) from brand (what the customer believes). A strong answer gives one example of each for the same company.

### Tangible and emotional identifiers

**Definition:** Tangible identifiers are packaging, colour, logo and trademark. Emotional identifiers are the feelings a brand attaches through its message.
**Class example:** The Starbucks logo alone signals the brand. Nike's "Just Do It" speaks to self-doubt and fear, and its ads show the struggle, not the shoe.
**Business example:** Cadbury's purple colour is so tied to the brand that Cadbury fought a long legal battle in the UK to protect its shade of purple for chocolate.

### Credibility and heritage

**Definition:** Credibility comes from consistency over years. Heritage value is credibility borrowed from origin or history.
**Class example:** Maruti is trusted for service and Tata for trust. Swiss chocolate and Swiss watches carry heritage value.
**Business example:** Royal Enfield's riders' community events, such as the annual Rider Mania in Goa, keep a cult following alive.

## Brand failure and recovery

**Definition:** Marketing myopia, a term from Theodore Levitt (1960), is defining your business by the product you make instead of the need you serve.
**Business example:** Kodak engineer Steve Sasson built the first digital camera in 1975, but Kodak protected its film business and filed for bankruptcy in 2012. BlackBerry dominated business phones until touchscreen smartphones took over after 2007.
**Business example:** Maggi was banned in India in June 2015 over lead-content claims. Nestlé relaunched it in November 2015 and won back market leadership, showing that decades of trust helped it recover.
**Business example:** Nirma grew with low-priced detergent. HUL answered with Wheel in 1987, a lower-priced brand created to fight Nirma.

## Founder lessons

### Steve Jobs, Apple

**Key idea:** Marketing creates value and branding needs consistency.
**Class example:** The 1997 "Think Different" ad showed no product at all, only people who changed the world, such as Gandhi, Martin Luther King and Picasso.
**Business example:** Apple's design, stores and ads have stayed consistent for decades, which lets it charge premium prices.

### Howard Schultz, Starbucks

**Definition:** The "third place" is a social space between home and work, copied from French and Italian cafes.
**Class example:** His rule is authenticity. Tata runs Starbucks in India.
**Business example:** Tata Starbucks opened its first Indian store in Mumbai in 2012 as a 50:50 joint venture. The stores kept the third-place design but added Indian menu items.

### Phil Knight, Nike

**Key idea:** Know the customer's pulse.
**Class example:** Nike began as Blue Ribbon Sports, reselling shoes as a commodity. Read *Shoe Dog*.
**Business example:** Blue Ribbon Sports was founded in 1964 and became Nike in 1971. "Just Do It" arrived in 1988 with agency Wieden+Kennedy.

## Anything can be branded

**Class example:** Brands become verbs ("Google it", "Amazon it"). People (a celebrity chef), commodities (diamonds, coffee), NGOs (UNICEF), places and countries can all be branded.
**Business example:** De Beers' "A Diamond Is Forever" line (1947) turned a commodity stone into an emotional symbol of marriage.
**Business example:** Kerala Tourism's "God's Own Country" line has branded a state for decades.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Differentiate a product from a brand, with an example.
**A:** A product is what the company makes; a brand is the image and emotion the customer holds about it. A cup of coffee is a product, but Starbucks is a brand because customers link it to a third place, premium quality and a recognisable logo.

**Q2.** What is marketing myopia? Explain using Kodak.
**A:** Marketing myopia is defining a business by its product instead of the need it serves. Kodak saw itself as a film company, so it held back on the digital camera it invented in 1975 and later went bankrupt in 2012.

**Q3.** Explain Starbucks' "third place" concept and how it was adapted in India.
**A:** The third place is a social space between home and work. Tata Starbucks kept the relaxed store design when it entered India in 2012 but adapted the menu with local items.

**Q4.** How do tangible and emotional identifiers work together? Use Nike.
**A:** Nike's swoosh is a tangible identifier that makes the brand recognisable. "Just Do It" is an emotional identifier that speaks to self-doubt. Together they make the brand both visible and meaningful.

**Q5.** Show, with two examples, that a commodity or place can be branded.
**A:** De Beers turned diamonds into a symbol of lasting love with "A Diamond Is Forever". Kerala Tourism branded a state as "God's Own Country", giving travellers an emotional reason to choose it.

**Q6.** How did Maggi recover from its 2015 ban?
**A:** Nestlé withdrew the product, proved safety through tests and relaunched in November 2015. Decades of trust with Indian families helped Maggi regain its leading share.

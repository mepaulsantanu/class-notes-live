This Saturday SBM class covered how psychology and neuromarketing shape branding, then taught brand mind mapping as a tool to see how consumers perceive a brand. Most of the session explained CIA 1, where groups build aided and unaided mind maps for one B2C brand, and ended with groups choosing brands and drafting survey questions.

## Psychology and neuromarketing

### How neuromarketing works

**Definition:** Neuromarketing studies the brain's and body's responses to marketing, so brands can design elements that work below conscious thought.
**Class example:** The instructor runs an in-house psychology lab and has worked on branding for DRDO and Sunburn, and wrote a book on neuromarketing, *Brand Rhythm*. Agencies test consumers in labs to find exact trigger points before finalising an ad.

### Tools

1. **Eye tracking:** where the eye lands first on an ad or pack.
2. **Pupil dilation:** an involuntary reaction to a stimulus.
3. **Brain sensors (called fMRI in class):** read brain activity directly.

**Class example:** Results shape taglines, visuals and colour palettes.
**Business example:** Frito-Lay used EEG-based neuromarketing research in 2008 on Cheetos. It found people enjoyed the orange cheese dust left on their fingers, and built an ad campaign around it.
**Exam tip:** Name the three tools together, then link each to a branding output: eye tracking to layout, pupil dilation to visuals, brain sensors to overall emotional response.

### Thought to action

**Formula:** Thoughts → Words → Action (as stated in class).
**Class example:** Branding uses attraction to steer what a customer sees and reads, so it shapes the first step, thought.
**Remember:** Positive wiring widens perception; negative wiring narrows it.

## Retail visual triggers

**Definition:** Retailers use decor, colour and layout to trigger feelings such as premium, urgency or festivity.
**Class example:** Shoppers Stop uses black and white decor for a premium feel. Reliance uses festive window displays to drive footfall. Woodlands has a distinct planogram and navigation.
**Business example:** Apple stores use bright light, wood tables and open space so products feel premium and easy to try. The layout invites you to touch.

## Brand mind mapping

**Definition:** A brand mind map shows every association a consumer links to a brand, grouped as functional (what it does), emotional (how it feels) and imagery (what it looks like).
**Class example (McDonald's):** Golden Arches, then McCafe and McFlurry, consistent quality, a family and kids mantra (Happy Meal), and Ronald McDonald House for social image.
**Class example (Starbucks):** Customisation (functional), third place (emotional), premium and the mermaid logo (imagery).
**Business example:** Keller calls these "brand associations". Strong, favourable and unique associations are what build customer-based brand equity (textbook).

### Case: Aadhaar as a brand

**Class example:** Associations included KYC, biometrics, photograph and authenticity. It is seen as a basic necessity, at the base of Maslow's hierarchy, and linked with trust, security and inclusion.
**Business example:** UIDAI issued the first Aadhaar numbers in 2010 under Nandan Nilekani, and over 1.3 billion have been issued since. It shows that a government service can build brand associations like any product.

### Research methods

1. **Unaided mapping:** informal, open questions capture raw, spontaneous opinion.
2. **Aided mapping:** structured questionnaires and product trials reveal deeper psychology.
3. **Gap analysis:** compare Map A (unaided) with Map B (aided) to find where perception differs.

**Remember:** Seniors used AI to turn interview transcripts into decision trees and sentiment maps.
**Exam tip:** For any brand research design, list unaided first, then aided, then gap analysis. Unaided shows what is top of mind; aided shows what people know when prompted.

### Worked examples

**Class example:** Nandini (dairy range, strong in Karnataka, value buyers), Lenskart (pain points) and sneaker brands.
**Business example:** Nandini, run by the Karnataka Milk Federation, is the dominant dairy brand in Karnataka. It sponsored South Africa and Scotland at the 2024 T20 World Cup to raise its profile.
**Business example:** Lenskart started online in 2010, then opened stores and home eye tests because many buyers did not trust buying glasses online. It redesigned the journey around that pain point.

## CIA 1 steps

1. Choose one B2C brand, in groups of up to 5.
2. List 5 main elements as words, images and feelings.
3. Branch them into functional, emotional, symbolic, experiential and visual associations.
4. Mark each association as strong, unique, positive or negative.
5. Recommend what the brand should change, communicate or reinforce.

**Class example:** The faculty suggested treating it as a consulting rebrand of the corporate or umbrella brand.

### Your group's brand options

**Class example:** The group discussed MuscleBlaze, Comet, Crocs (dropped as too familiar), Pidilite/Fevicol and Marvel. Survey segments: students and teachers, professionals (carpenters for Pidilite) and dealers.
**Class example:** Draft Marvel questions: first three things that come to mind, feelings linked to Marvel, what it represents, your first Marvel film, and "if Marvel were a person, describe it".
**Business example:** Pidilite is a classic umbrella brand. Fevicol leads Indian adhesives, and its "Fevicol ka mazboot jod" ads made a glue brand a household name, which gives strong associations to map.
**Business example:** Disney bought Marvel in 2009 for about $4 billion. The Marvel films since 2008 have grossed over $29 billion worldwide, so Marvel has rich emotional and imagery associations to study.

## What the faculty stressed

- Map what people think before you plan a rebrand.
- Always run both unaided and aided mapping, then compare.
- Group associations as functional, emotional and imagery.
- Treat CIA 1 as a real consulting brief.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** What is neuromarketing? Name three tools and one real example.
**A:** Neuromarketing measures brain and body responses to marketing. Tools include eye tracking, pupil dilation and brain sensors. Frito-Lay's Cheetos research found people liked the cheese dust on their fingers and built a campaign around it.

**Q2.** Distinguish aided from unaided brand mapping and explain gap analysis.
**A:** Unaided mapping uses open questions to capture what comes to mind without prompting. Aided mapping uses structured questions or trials. Gap analysis compares the two maps to find where spontaneous perception differs from prompted knowledge.

**Q3.** Build a mind map outline for Starbucks using the three association groups.
**A:** Functional: customisation, consistent quality. Emotional: the third place, comfort. Imagery: the mermaid logo, green branding, premium store design.

**Q4.** How can a government service like Aadhaar be treated as a brand?
**A:** Aadhaar carries associations such as KYC, biometrics, trust, security and inclusion. It meets a basic need, and over 1.3 billion people hold it, so it has recall and perception like any brand.

**Q5.** Outline the five steps of SBM CIA 1.
**A:** Choose a B2C brand; list 5 main elements as words, images and feelings; branch them into functional, emotional, symbolic, experiential and visual associations; mark each as strong, unique, positive or negative; and recommend what to change, communicate or reinforce.

**Q6.** Why is Pidilite a good choice for an umbrella-brand mind map?
**A:** Pidilite covers Fevicol, Fevikwik and other products, so associations can be traced from the corporate brand to each product. Fevicol's famous "mazboot jod" ads give strong emotional and imagery associations to map.

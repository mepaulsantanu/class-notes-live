This E&I class looked at the entrepreneurial mindset: tolerance for ambiguity, grit, and seeing an entrepreneur as a problem solver. It used stories of Rupert Murdoch, Slack, Kunal Shah and Flipkart's Ekart, debated who counts as an entrepreneur, and set a 350-word go-to-market assignment.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Class example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tolerance for ambiguity | Comfort acting without full information | Google placing people in roles to suit this |
| Grit | Passion and persistence toward long-term goals | Setting ambitious but reachable goals |
| Pivot | Changing a business's direction based on what works | Glitch becoming Slack |
| Serial entrepreneur | Someone who starts several ventures | Kunal Shah |
| Captive logistics | A company running its own delivery arm | Flipkart's Ekart |
| Go-to-market (GTM) plan | How a product will reach and win its first customers | The 350-word assignment |

## Personality and ambiguity

**Definition:** Tolerance for ambiguity is the ability to act and stay calm without full information.
**Class example:** Some people need structure and dislike uncertainty; others tolerate it. Google places people in roles that fit this.
**Business example:** Early-stage startups change plans often. Founders who need certainty tend to struggle in the first years.
**Exam tip:** List tolerance for ambiguity alongside risk-taking and need for achievement when answering on entrepreneurial traits.

## India's changing mindset

**Class example:** India's mindset has shifted since last year. More founders restart after losses. The class asked what share of Indians run a business, and 6% to 10% was guessed.
**Business example:** Byju's rise and fall and Paytm's regulatory troubles did not stop new founders. India remains one of the world's largest startup bases, with over 100 unicorns created by the mid-2020s.

## Grit

**Definition:** Grit is passion and persistence toward long-term goals. Psychologist Angela Duckworth popularised the term.
**Class example:** Keep wanting more, but set goals that are ambitious and still reachable.
**Business example:** Zerodha's Nithin Kamath worked as a trader for years before starting Zerodha in 2010. It grew into one of India's largest stockbrokers without taking outside funding.

## Founder stories

### Rupert Murdoch

**Class example:** Murdoch took over his father's newspaper in his early twenties and built a media empire, including a US news channel Donald Trump favours.
**Business example:** He inherited the Adelaide News in 1952 after his father died, then built News Corp. It came to include Fox News, The Wall Street Journal and The Times.

### Who counts as an entrepreneur?

**Class example:** The class debated Elon Musk, Donald Trump and Virat Kohli. The Uber series mentioned was *Super Pumped*.
**Key idea:** An entrepreneur is a problem solver, and "the improviser is the best".
**Business example:** Virat Kohli is a co-owner of the clothing brand Wrogn and invested in brands such as Digit Insurance. The debate is whether celebrity founders build businesses or lend their names to them.

### Slack came from a failed game

**Definition:** A pivot is changing direction when the original plan is not working but part of it is.
**Class example:** Glitch was a gaming company that built an internal chat tool, which became Slack.
**Business example:** Stewart Butterfield's company Tiny Speck shut down the game Glitch in 2012 and launched Slack in 2014. Salesforce bought Slack in 2021 for about $27.7 billion. Butterfield had pivoted before: Flickr also grew out of an online game.

### Kunal Shah

**Class example:** Kunal Shah's early ventures moved from wire transfers (sold for about 1 million) to mobile value-added services, which smartphones then made obsolete.
**Business example:** He co-founded FreeCharge, which Snapdeal bought in 2015 for about $400 million, then founded CRED in 2018.
**Remember:** Serial entrepreneurs learn from each venture. A business made obsolete by technology can still teach the next one.

### Flipkart's Ekart

**Definition:** Captive logistics means a company runs its own delivery network instead of outsourcing it.
**Class example:** Flipkart's logistics arm is Ekart. Zomato came up on partnerships.
**Business example:** Flipkart built Ekart because third-party couriers could not reliably handle cash-on-delivery and last-mile delivery in India. Owning delivery gave it control over customer experience.

## What the faculty stressed

- Entrepreneurs solve problems and improvise.
- Grit and tolerance for ambiguity separate founders who last.
- Pivots are normal; many big companies started as something else.

## Assignments and deadlines

- Handwritten plan of about 350 words covering go-to-market, marketing, funding and user acquisition, due end of day 11 Sep.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** What is tolerance for ambiguity, and why does it matter for founders?
**A:** It is the ability to act calmly without full information. Early-stage ventures change plans often, so founders who need certainty struggle, while those who tolerate ambiguity can keep deciding and moving.

**Q2.** Explain grit with an Indian business example.
**A:** Grit is passion and persistence toward long-term goals. Nithin Kamath spent years as a trader before starting Zerodha in 2010 and grew it without outside funding into one of India's largest stockbrokers.

**Q3.** What is a pivot? Use Slack.
**A:** A pivot is changing direction when the original plan fails but part of it works. Tiny Speck's game Glitch failed, but its internal chat tool became Slack in 2014, and Salesforce later bought it for about $27.7 billion.

**Q4.** Why did Flipkart build its own logistics arm?
**A:** Third-party couriers could not reliably handle cash on delivery and last-mile delivery in India. Ekart gave Flipkart control over delivery speed and customer experience.

**Q5.** Is a celebrity who starts a brand an entrepreneur? Discuss.
**A:** Yes, if they take real risk and help build the business, as with Virat Kohli's stake in Wrogn. Less so if they only lend their name. The test is whether they solve a problem and carry risk.

**Q6.** Outline what a go-to-market plan should cover.
**A:** The target customer, the value proposition, pricing, channels for reaching customers, the marketing plan, how users will be acquired early, and how the launch will be funded.

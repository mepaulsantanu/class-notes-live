## The 5M framework of advertising management

**Definition:** The 5Ms are the five core decisions in managing advertising. Mission is the objective or goal of the ad. Money is the budget. Message covers the creative side and tactics. Medium is channel selection and media planning. Measurement is testing how effective the ad was.
**Class example:** The faculty presented the 5Ms as the structure of the advertising unit, starting with Mission and ending with Measurement.
**Business example:** When Reliance Jio launched in 2016, the mission was mass awareness of free voice and cheap data, the money was a very large launch budget, the message centred on "digital life", the medium mix covered newspapers, TV and outdoor, and measurement came through subscriber sign-ups (over 100 million users within about six months).
**Remember:** Mission, Money, Message, Medium, Measurement. The order follows the logic of planning: decide the goal first and measure last. (Kotler, textbook)
**Exam tip:** In an applied question, walk through all five Ms for the given brand. One line per M is better than skipping any.

## Measurement: testing ad effectiveness

### Pre-testing and post-testing

**Definition:** Pre-testing checks an ad before it is released, so you can fix weak ads before spending on media. Post-testing checks the ad after release, to see whether it met its objective.
**Class example:** The faculty linked both kinds of testing to the Measurement M of the 5M framework.
**Business example:** Kantar's LINK copy test is widely used by Indian FMCG advertisers to pre-test TVCs on recall, branding and persuasion before they go on air.

### Physiological (mechanical) testing

**Definition:** Instruments record the body's involuntary response to an ad, which the viewer cannot fake.
**Class example:** The pupillometer measures pupil dilation when a viewer sees a strong visual stimulus. Blood pressure and body temperature also change under high-intensity sound or visuals, such as IMAX effects or a high-decibel seminar.
**Business example:** Neuromarketing labs (for example Nielsen Consumer Neuroscience) use eye tracking, skin response and EEG to test which frame of an ad grabs attention.
**Remember:** Other mechanical tools include the galvanic skin response meter and the eye camera (textbook).

### Digital measurement

**Definition:** On digital platforms, effectiveness is measured through tracked behaviour such as screen time, watch time, reach and engagement.
**Class example:** Screen time tracking on Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn.
**Business example:** Meta Ads Manager shows advertisers reach, average watch time and ThruPlays for every reel ad.

## The concept of a continuum

**Definition:** A continuum is a spectrum where the boundaries are hazy and one end merges gradually into the other. Most real cases sit somewhere in between, not at either end.
**Class example:** The colours of a rainbow (VIBGYOR): you cannot point to the exact spot where blue ends and green begins.
**Business example:** See the two continuums below.

### Goods-service continuum

**Definition:** It runs from pure goods (tangible) to pure services (intangible). Most offerings fall in between and mix both.
**Class example:** The faculty used it to show that most products are partly a good and partly a service.
**Business example:** Tata Salt sits near the pure-goods end. A McKinsey consulting project sits near the pure-service end. A Domino's order sits in the middle: a pizza (good) plus 30-minute delivery (service).

### Telling-selling continuum

**Definition:** It runs from telling (informing the audience) to selling (persuading and convincing the audience). Every ad sits somewhere on this line.
**Class example:** Recruitment and educational ads used to be pure telling: formal notices listing posts and eligibility. Today they combine telling with selling, attracting talent and projecting the organisation as an ideal workplace.
**Business example:** The Indian Army's recruitment line "Do you have it in you?" moved from a plain vacancy notice to a pitch that sells pride, adventure and a career.
**Exam tip:** If asked to classify an ad, place it on the telling-selling line and justify the position. Do not force it into one extreme.

## Mission: advertising objectives

### Writing an objective

**Definition:** An advertising objective is a specific communication task to be achieved with a target audience in a given time.
**Class example:** The faculty insisted that every objective statement must begin with the word "to", for example "to inform", "to persuade", "to remind".
**Business example:** "To make 60% of urban 18 to 30 year olds aware of the new variant within three months" is a well-written objective.
**Remember:** Start with "to", then a verb.

### Objectives across the product life cycle (PLC)

**Definition:** The PLC graph plots Time on the X-axis (independent variable) against Sales on the Y-axis (dependent variable). The ad objective changes with the stage.
**Formula:** Sales = f(Time). X-axis: Time. Y-axis: Sales.

**Introduction stage: to inform**
**Definition:** The goal is to inform and spread awareness of a new product or category.
**Class example:** At launch, people do not know the product exists, so the ad must tell them.
**Business example:** Jio's 2016 launch campaign told people about free calls and its 4G network.

**Growth stage: to persuade**
**Definition:** Competitors enter, so the ad must persuade and prove superiority over rivals.
**Class example:** The faculty described this as the stage of proving you are better than the competition.
**Business example:** HUL's Rin ran a comparative ad in 2010 showing it whiter than Tide, which led P&G to court. This is persuasive advertising at its sharpest.

**Maturity stage (early and late): to remind**
**Definition:** The brand is known. The ad reminds and reinforces to keep mind space.
**Class example:** The faculty split maturity into early and late maturity, with reminder and reinforcement as the goal in both.
**Business example:** Amul's topical hoardings and Parle-G's continued advertising keep long-established brands in mind.

**Decline stage**
**Definition:** The final stage, where sales fall.
**Class example:** Named as the last stage of the cycle.
**Business example:** Brands in decline often cut spend sharply and only remind loyal users, or reposition. (textbook)

**Exam tip:** Draw the PLC curve with labelled axes and write the objective under each stage. A neat diagram earns marks.

### DAGMAR model

**Definition:** DAGMAR stands for Defining Advertising Goals for Measured Advertising Results. Russell H. Colley set it out in his 1961 book of the same name. It says every advertisement should have a clearly defined goal that leads to a measurable result.
**Class example:** The faculty noted that the idea is sound but often hard to achieve in real practice, because isolating the result of one ad is difficult.
**Business example:** A D2C brand running Instagram ads can apply DAGMAR closely: the goal is "to get 10,000 app installs in 30 days" and the result is measured exactly through tracked installs.
**Remember:** DAGMAR uses the communication spectrum Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action (ACCA) (textbook).
**Exam tip:** Mention the criticism the faculty gave: measurable results are hard to separate from other influences on sales.

## Medium: reach, frequency and impact

**Definition:** Three yardsticks compare advertising media. Reach is the total number of people the message gets to. Frequency is the number of times those people are exposed to it. Impact is the qualitative side: how strongly the ad registers.
**Class example:** Television ranks number one on all three, which is why the faculty called it the most powerful advertising medium.
**Business example:** Surf Excel's "Daag achhe hain" campaign has run on TV for years, giving HUL national reach, repeated exposure across serials and cricket, and the emotional impact of the children's stories.
**Formula:** Gross Rating Points (GRP) = Reach (%) × Average frequency (textbook).
**Exam tip:** When you justify a media choice, score the medium on reach, frequency and impact one by one.

### Doordarshan versus cable and satellite

**Definition:** State-run Doordarshan gives the deepest reach into India, while private cable and satellite channels add the impact.
**Class example:** Doordarshan is essential for reaching interior parts of the country, small towns and villages. It delivers high reach and frequency, but its impact can be lower because people see it as an outdated platform. Cable and satellite channels make up for that lower impact.
**Business example:** FMCG brands chasing rural buyers buy slots on DD Free Dish, the free-to-air DTH service that reaches tens of millions of rural homes, and pair it with Hindi entertainment channels such as Star Plus for impact.

## Classification of advertising

### Television commercials (TVCs)

**Definition:** A television commercial is an ad aired in designated commercial breaks during TV serials and programmes.
**Class example:** Ad lengths come in multiples of 10 seconds, such as 10, 30 or 50 seconds. A one-minute ad is generally considered too long.
**Business example:** Most Indian FMCG TVCs run as a 30-second main edit with a 10 or 15-second cut-down for reminder flights.
**Remember:** TV is still the costliest form of advertising because of its massive reach.

### Prime time

**Definition:** The window of maximum viewership, and so the costliest slot.
**Class example:** In India, prime time is 7:00 pm to 9:00 pm. OTT is growing, yet satellite TV and Doordarshan still dominate mainstream India and rural areas.
**Business example:** Big shows like Kaun Banega Crorepati and Bigg Boss air in the evening slots and command the highest ad rates on their channels.

### Roadblock advertising

**Definition:** The same ad is aired on many channels at the same time, so the viewer cannot avoid it by switching channels.
**Class example:** Even if you surf from one channel to another during the break, you meet the same ad.
**Business example:** Large advertisers such as HUL and P&G book roadblocks across Hindi general entertainment channels in prime time for major launches.
**Exam tip:** Roadblocks buy reach and certainty of exposure. Expect a question on why a brand would pay for one.

### Communication synergy

**Definition:** The ads aired in a show's break feature the same actors who appear in that show, so the programme and the ad support each other.
**Class example:** Commercials in a serial's break starring the serial's own actors.
**Business example:** During Kaun Banega Crorepati, ads for brands endorsed by host Amitabh Bachchan, such as Cadbury Dairy Milk, ran in the show's breaks.
**Remember:** The Colors channel, launched after 2000 (in 2008), grew rapidly on reality shows like Bigg Boss.

### Exaggeration in TV ads: "Pakhuri" (likely puffery)

**Definition:** The faculty called it "Pakhuri" (the standard term is most likely puffery). It means using exaggerated effects in communication to drive the point home. The exaggeration is obvious enough that no reasonable viewer takes it literally.
**Class example:** Toothpaste ads with children whose teeth look unnaturally perfect, and hair commercials with exaggerated results. The faculty cited the Colgate Visible White ad featuring Abhishek and Preity Zinta, where the teeth look unreal compared with real-life standards.
**Business example:** Fevicol's ads, such as the overloaded truck or bus where nobody falls off, use wild exaggeration to make "strong bond" memorable. Red Bull's "gives you wings" is the classic global case of puffery.
**Remember:** Puffery works best on television because it depends on visual effects. It falls flat on radio, where the listener cannot see the exaggerated result.
**Exam tip:** Separate harmless puffery from a misleading factual claim. In India, ASCI's code allows obvious exaggeration but not false claims about the product (textbook).

### Commercials on OTT platforms

**Definition:** Ad-supported OTT plans now insert commercial breaks in streamed content, much like TV.
**Class example:** Zee5, Jio and Hotstar (now merged as JioHotstar) all run commercial breaks. Netflix is the main exception.
**Business example:** JioHotstar sells ad slots during IPL streaming, while Netflix in India runs on subscription with no ad tier.

### OTT and the creator talent pipeline

**Definition:** Digital creators who build audiences on YouTube are moving to mainstream OTT shows, which also makes them faces of the ad campaigns built around those shows.
**Class example:** Bhuvan Bam began on YouTube and moved to OTT with Taaza Khabar on Disney+ Hotstar. The class also discussed his role in "The Revolutionary" (likely The Revolutionaries) and its ad campaigns. TVF (The Viral Fever) was named as a launchpad for grounded artists such as Jitendra Kumar, through shows like Panchayat and Kota Factory.
**Business example:** Kota Factory started on TVF's YouTube channel in 2019 and later moved to Netflix, while Panchayat runs on Amazon Prime Video. Jitendra Kumar's relatable, small-town image from these shows has made him a face for brand ads.

### Radio advertising

**Definition:** Audio ads and paid mentions on radio stations. Radio was reinvented in India in the late 1990s through FM (Frequency Modulation), replacing the older AM (Amplitude Modulation) as the popular band.
**Class example:** AM channels still reach remote areas such as mountains, where there is no cellular network. The faculty stressed radio's psychological advantage: TV dictates the visual, but radio leaves the listener free to imagine. RJ mentions are paid shout-outs woven into the RJ's conversation instead of a formal ad spot.
**Business example:** Private FM took off in 2001 with Radio City in Bengaluru, followed by Radio Mirchi. Local brands such as jewellers and real estate developers regularly pay RJs for live reads during morning drive-time shows.
**Remember:** Puffery suits TV, while imagination suits radio. Pick the medium that fits the creative idea.

### Infomercials and advertorials

**Definition:** An infomercial is a hybrid of information and a commercial: a long-format ad that explains and demonstrates the product. An advertorial is a hybrid of an advertisement and an editorial: paid content designed to look like part of the newspaper's own editorial matter.
**Class example:** Full-page newspaper spreads on a Chief Minister's achievements in the first 100 days. The faculty separated advertorials from newspaper inserts: inserts are pamphlets or leaflets slipped in by the hawker, while an advertorial is printed inside the paper in editorial style.
**Business example:** Teleshopping channels such as TSN (Telebrands) run half-hour infomercials demonstrating fitness gadgets and kitchen tools. The Times of India carries paid stories labelled "Consumer Connect Initiative", a standard advertorial format.
**Exam tip:** Use the word pairs to remember them: info + commercial, advertisement + editorial.

### Product, corporate and dealer ads

**Definition:** Brand advertising can be classified by what or who it promotes. Product ads focus on a specific product. Corporate or group ads build the identity of the whole group. Dealer or retailer ads are issued by the dealer to promote a specific outlet and location.
**Class example:** A new series promoted on Star Plus is a product ad, while an ad for the Star TV network as a whole, or for the Aditya Birla Group, is a corporate ad. Automobile ads such as Tata Motors' often list local dealer addresses at the bottom, which is the dealer element.
**Business example:** Tata Group's corporate campaigns build trust in the Tata name across companies, while a Maruti Suzuki Arena dealer in Bengaluru runs its own newspaper ad with festive offers and its showroom address.

### Out-of-home (OOH) and point-of-sale advertising

**Definition:** Ads that reach people outside their homes, in public spaces and at the point of purchase.
**Class example:**
- Billboards and hoardings: their effectiveness is hard to assess, and they can be a public safety hazard if they fall.
- Pavement and street ads: banners or creative changes to roads, such as McDonald's modifying zebra crossings.
- Digital electronic posters: used heavily on campuses to target a captive audience of students and staff.
- In-store ads: promotions inside a point of purchase such as an Airtel store or Croma.
- ATM ads: shown on the screen while the transaction runs.

**Business example:** Amul's topical hoardings have run since 1966 and remain India's best-known OOH campaign. The safety risk is real: in May 2024 a huge illegal hoarding collapsed in Ghatkopar, Mumbai, killing 17 people.

### Transit advertising

**Definition:** Ads placed on vehicles (mobile transit) or at transport hubs (stationary transit).
**Class example:** Mobile transit covers BEST buses in Mumbai, auto-rickshaws and, increasingly, Indian Railways. Stationary transit covers metro stations, railway stations, highways and subways. The faculty cited Flipkart's live-captured video ads in metros as a creative case.
**Business example:** Mumbai's suburban local trains carry full-body brand wraps, so a single train shows the ad to commuters at every station on the line.

## Special advertising techniques

### Surrogate advertising

**Definition:** Using a substitute product that carries the same brand name to promote a product whose advertising is prohibited, such as alcohol or tobacco.
**Class example:** Liquor brands advertise soda, club glasses or music CDs instead of the drink. Imperial Blue's "Men will be men" campaign builds the brand through humour and male bonding without showing whisky.
**Business example:** Kingfisher promotes packaged drinking water and soda, and Royal Stag ran "Royal Stag Mega Music" CDs. In 2022 Vimal pan masala ads with Shah Rukh Khan, Ajay Devgn and Akshay Kumar drew backlash as surrogates for tobacco.
**Remember:** India bans direct liquor and tobacco ads on TV under the Cable TV Networks Rules, which is why surrogates exist (textbook).

### Stealth advertising (product placement)

**Definition:** Branded products are used by actors in films or shows without being named or pitched.
**Class example:** Apple products in Hollywood films, Samsung phones in Korean dramas and Kia cars in Drishyam 2.
**Business example:** Coca-Cola placed its product in the Hindi film Taal (1999), and Apple's MacBooks and iPhones appear in many Indian web series.

### Subliminal advertising

**Definition:** Messages that target the subconscious mind, registering without the viewer consciously noticing.
**Class example:** The Amazon logo's arrow runs from A to Z, signalling that Amazon sells everything. The FedEx logo hides an arrow between the E and the x, signalling speed and movement.
**Business example:** Baskin-Robbins hides "31" in the pink parts of its BR logo, a nod to its 31 flavours.
**Remember:** Strictly subliminal ads (flashed below conscious perception) are contested and restricted. Hidden-meaning logos are the safe, everyday form (textbook).

## Government and political advertising

**Definition:** Government ads are issued by ministries for public programmes. Political ads are issued by parties and are often highly creative work by professional agencies.
**Class example:** Pulse Polio is the government example. For political ads, the faculty cited "India Shining", the NDA government's 2004 campaign, credited in class to Ogilvy. It was a creative success, yet the NDA lost the election, proving creativity alone does not win votes.
**Business example:** The Pulse Polio campaign "Do boond zindagi ki" with Amitabh Bachchan helped India become polio-free (certified in 2014). Note that India Shining is usually credited to Grey Worldwide; Ogilvy's Piyush Pandey created BJP's 2014 line "Ab ki baar Modi sarkar".
**Exam tip:** Use India Shining to argue that an ad cannot fix a gap between the message and voters' lived experience.

## History of Indian television (context for TVCs)

### Serials and the middle class

**Definition:** A product or medium is established once it reaches middle-class households. The upper class adopts first, but middle-class penetration signals mainstream success.
**Class example:** TV serials became prominent in India in the 1980s. Later came the era of Ekta Kapoor and Balaji Telefilms, known for high drama and heavy use of music.
**Business example:** The Maruti 800 became the symbol of the car going mainstream once middle-class families could buy it.

### Colour TV and landmark broadcasts

**Definition:** Landmark events drive adoption of a new media format.
**Class example:** India hosted the Asian Games in 1982, the first major event telecast in colour across India. Colour TV went mainstream only in the 1990s, and some families got a colour set as late as 1991. Ramayan was telecast around 1987. India also hosted the first Asian Games in 1951 in New Delhi, four years after independence.
**Business example:** Doordarshan began colour transmission for the 1982 Asian Games, and Ramayan (1987 to 1988) drew huge audiences that made TV a family purchase.

### Origin of soap operas

**Definition:** Daily soaps or soap operas began in the United States. They were called soaps because soap manufacturers sponsored them, and India later copied this sponsorship model.
**Class example:** The faculty traced the name to soap company sponsorship.
**Business example:** In the US, Procter & Gamble sponsored radio serials such as "Ma Perkins" (for Oxydol). In India, Hum Log (1984), the first Indian soap, was sponsored by Nestlé's Maggi noodles.

### Vertical shows

**Definition:** Serialised stories told in one-minute vertical episodes (reels), released daily for mobile viewing, unlike the horizontal frame of film and TV.
**Class example:** Vertical shows on Instagram.
**Business example:** Kuku TV runs paid vertical micro-drama series made for phone screens.

### Cultural lag in media trends

**Definition:** A media trend that emerges in the US usually takes about 20 years to become mainstream in India, though India sometimes leads.
**Class example:** The roughly 20-year gap, with WhatsApp usage as the exception where India leads in scale.
**Business example:** India is WhatsApp's largest market, with more than 500 million users. Reality TV went the other way: Big Brother (1999) reached India as Bigg Boss in 2006.

## What the faculty stressed

- Learn the 5Ms in order: Mission, Money, Message, Medium, Measurement.
- Every objective statement must start with "to".
- Link each PLC stage to its objective: introduction to inform, growth to persuade, maturity to remind.
- Draw the PLC graph with Time on the X-axis and Sales on the Y-axis.
- DAGMAR is the ideal, but measured results are hard to achieve in practice.
- A continuum has hazy boundaries; most ads and products sit between the ends.

- Puffery suits TV because it needs visuals; radio works through the listener's imagination.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Explain the 5M framework of advertising management with an example.
**A:** The 5Ms are Mission (objective), Money (budget), Message (creative and tactics), Medium (channel and media planning) and Measurement (testing effectiveness). For Jio's 2016 launch, the mission was awareness, a large budget backed it, the message was "digital life", the media were TV, print and outdoor, and sign-ups measured success.

**Q2.** How do advertising objectives change across the product life cycle?
**A:** In the introduction stage, the objective is to inform and build awareness. In growth, it is to persuade and prove superiority over competitors. In early and late maturity, it is to remind and reinforce the brand to hold mind space, and in decline, spend is usually cut.

**Q3.** What is DAGMAR? Why is it hard to apply in practice?
**A:** DAGMAR, from Russell H. Colley's book, stands for Defining Advertising Goals for Measured Advertising Results. It says every ad needs a clearly defined goal with a measurable result. In practice, sales are influenced by price, distribution and competitors as well, so isolating what one ad achieved is difficult.

**Q4.** Why is television called the most powerful advertising medium? Where does Doordarshan fit in?
**A:** Television ranks first on reach (how many people see the ad), frequency (how often they see it) and impact (how strongly it registers). Doordarshan carries the message into villages and small towns with high reach and frequency, but its impact is lower because viewers see it as outdated. Advertisers therefore pair it with cable and satellite channels, and can add roadblocks in prime time for certain exposure.

**Q5.** Distinguish surrogate, stealth and subliminal advertising with examples.
**A:** Surrogate advertising promotes a banned product through a substitute carrying the same brand, such as liquor brands selling soda, glasses or music CDs, or Imperial Blue's "Men will be men". Stealth advertising places a branded product on screen without naming it, as with Kia in Drishyam 2. Subliminal advertising targets the subconscious, like the hidden arrows in the Amazon and FedEx logos.

**Q6.** How is an advertorial different from an infomercial and from a newspaper insert?
**A:** An advertorial blends an advertisement with an editorial: it is paid content laid out to look like the newspaper's own reporting, such as a spread on a Chief Minister's first 100 days. An infomercial blends information with a commercial, usually a long TV demonstration. An insert is a separate pamphlet slipped into the paper by the hawker, so it never looks like editorial content.
